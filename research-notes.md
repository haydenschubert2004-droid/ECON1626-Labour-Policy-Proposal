# Evidence and policy-design checks

Working research record, 25 September 2026. This is supporting material, not the submitted 1,200-word brief. Verified descriptions, economic interpretation and proposed design assumptions are separated below.

## Research question and decision

Should DEWR supplement existing AI skills provision with a targeted trial of paid learning time and workplace application for general clerks under Action 5 of the National AI Plan?

Recommend developing a bounded trial, not asserting that a national subsidy already has proven benefits. General clerks provide a manageable occupational scope; restrict access by demonstrated task change and unmet need rather than assume every exposed worker needs assistance. There is not yet sufficient evidence here to restrict eligibility to small businesses.

## Economic case to test

- Transferable skills can benefit future employers as well as the firm paying for training, potentially weakening employers' investment incentives.
- Time, information and coordination constraints can prevent useful training even when content is free.
- Neither mechanism establishes the size of underinvestment among general clerks. Screen for unmet need and measure additional participation.
- Productivity may increase without wage gains if bargaining power or outside options are weak. Include worker consultation, job quality and earnings in evaluation.
- AI could lower entry costs while data, complementary investment and vendor scale favour larger firms. Treat industrial concentration as a possible mechanism, not an observed result proved by these sources.
- Female and part-time workforce shares justify examining access and outcomes; they do not establish that every woman or part-time worker is disadvantaged.

## Fair comparison of three options

Use existing free training and services as the baseline over a common 24-month horizon. Do not give unsupported numerical scores or claim cost-effectiveness without outcome estimates.

| Option | Mechanism and target | Main advantage | Main limitation | Transparent cost basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Worker-directed assistance | Needs assessment and capped additional applied learning for employed general clerks with a demonstrated gap. | Worker choice and portable skills. | Paying course fees may leave time and workplace barriers unresolved. | Eligible participants × actual additional learning cost, plus delivery and evaluation; exclude free provision. |
| 2. Co-funded paid learning and workplace application | Employers release eligible clerks for transferable learning and consult workers on task changes. | Addresses time and application together. | Deadweight, employer selection and administrative burden; benefits are unproven. | Wage reimbursement plus separately justified learning/support, administration and evaluation. |
| 3. Transition assistance | Counselling, skills recognition, matching and bridging learning for documented role loss/change. | Supports mobility and workers outside supportive employers. | Suitable vacancies matter; it may arrive after disruption. | Clients × assessed service intensity, plus administration/evaluation; avoid duplicating employment services. |

Evaluate efficiency, equity, feasibility and incremental fiscal cost consistently. The third option serves a different need; equal participant counts do not imply equivalent benefits. Previous illustrative alternative totals are not validated comparative prices and should not determine the ranking. Obtain service-cost benchmarks before the final options table.

## Provisional option 2 budget: a ceiling, not a quotation

All amounts are Australian dollars. Scale, hours, subsidy rate and allowances are author-designed assumptions. The $40 hourly benchmark comes from the occupational profile but is not a loaded employer cost, prescribed wage or universal worker rate.

| Component | Proposed maximum calculation | Public funding |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Paid learning reimbursement | 1,000 workers × up to 20 hours × 50% × eligible hourly wage capped at $40 | $400,000 |
| Additional assessed learning | Up to $500 per worker, only where existing free provision cannot meet the diagnosed need | $500,000 |
| Workplace support | Planning allowance of $200 per worker; scope separate from learning to avoid double payment | $200,000 |
| Administration | Unbenchmarked planning allowance | $150,000 |
| Independent evaluation | Unbenchmarked planning allowance | $200,000 |
| Subtotal | Maximum of the above | $1,450,000 |
| Contingency | 10% of subtotal | $145,000 |
| **Maximum illustrative envelope** | **Subject to cost validation and design approval** | **$1,595,000** |

The $500 allowance must not be described as a tuition fee payable for every participant: free applied content exists. Actual approved expenditure could be substantially below this envelope. Employers meet remaining wages and applicable on-costs; at the assumed $40 rate and full hours their base-wage contribution is $400,000. Higher wages increase employer contributions under this cap.

Sensitivity, holding administration/evaluation at $350,000: varying the $1.1 million direct-support allowance by ±25%, then applying 10% contingency, yields $1,292,500–$1,897,500. This is a design sensitivity range, not a confidence interval or permission to exceed the proposed ceiling. No return-on-investment estimate is established.

## Implementation and evaluation assumptions

- Months 1–3: DEWR commissions overlap/needs assessment, provider pricing, worker consultation and independent evaluation design. Confirm delivery authority and data access; JSA is a proposed analytical adviser, not an assumed programme operator.
- Months 4–6: recruit eligible workplaces and workers; collect baseline skills, existing training plans, wages, hours and job-quality measures. Specify the occupational crosswalk if recruitment uses OSCA.
- Months 7–12: deliver learning and supervised application using existing resources where suitable; verify payroll and avoid duplicate claims.
- Months 13–24: collect six- and twelve-month follow-up where timing permits; publish findings and decide whether to stop, redesign or expand.
- Prefer randomised phased offers at employer level if feasible. Both groups retain ordinary access to existing services and legal entitlements. Account for employer clustering and spillovers.
- Commission statistical power calculations before finalising scale; 1,000 workers is a budget assumption, not proof of sufficient power.
- Primary outcomes: additional participation and independently assessed skill application. Secondary outcomes: earnings, hours, retention, workload and task quality. Report access/outcomes by gender and employment type where samples permit.
- Pre-register outcomes, analysis and expansion rules before delivery. Course completion alone cannot justify expansion; seek improvement over the baseline with acceptable cost and no material job-quality harm. Set numerical thresholds through design and costing rather than invent them here.
