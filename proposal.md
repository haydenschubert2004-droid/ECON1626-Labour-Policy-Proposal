# AI Documentation in England's NHS: Productivity and a Fair Workforce Transition

- **Policy client and context:** Advice to the Department of Health and Social Care on workforce arrangements for AI documentation tools under the 10 Year Health Plan for England.
- **Status:** First-draft bullet-point outline; not the final 1,200-word proposal.
- **Working recommendation:** A staged, independently evaluated rollout with targeted training and redeployment support; test this preference against the evidence.
- **Scope:** England; AI tools that produce draft clinical notes and letters, focusing on clinicians and affected administrative staff.
- **Research approach:** Verify policy status, programme overlap, source findings and cost assumptions before the full draft. Do not treat projected benefits as measured outcomes.

## Executive summary — target 130 words

- Define the policy problem: how to turn reduced documentation work into useful care capacity while managing workforce adjustment.
- Summarise the potential benefits and unequal effects across occupational groups.
- Compare three approaches: broad rollout, comprehensive workforce-supported rollout, and staged evaluation-led rollout.
- State the preferred package, proposed budget and expected outcomes once research and costing are complete.

## Problem definition and policy context — target 150 words

- Anchor the proposal in the health plan's digital and workforce agenda.
- Establish what AI documentation programmes already exist and identify the additional policy decision this brief addresses.
- Explain the possible case for intervention: uncertainty about effectiveness, training barriers and adjustment costs.
- Select a bounded implementation setting, such as outpatient documentation in a sample of NHS trusts, after assessing evidence.
- Establish baseline documentation workload, staffing and digital readiness using primary sources.

## Economic and distributional impacts — target 280 words

- **Tasks:** Examine substitution for routine documentation and complementarity with clinical judgement; include time spent checking and correcting AI output.
- **Employment:** Assess whether reduced labour time per consultation increases care capacity, slows recruitment, changes roles or displaces some administrative work.
- **Wages:** Explain why productivity gains need not translate automatically into higher pay; examine pay-setting institutions, skill demand and bargaining.
- **Working conditions:** Evaluate possible effects on workload, overtime, autonomy and retention.
- **Industry structure:** Examine technology supplier concentration, procurement scale, interoperability and the costs of changing suppliers.
- **Equity:** Compare consequences for clinicians and administrative staff, and for organisations with different resources and digital capabilities.
- Distinguish task exposure from actual job loss and assess alternative explanations for observed workforce changes.

## Policy options and evaluation — target 240 words

- Use existing arrangements as the baseline; specify what each option adds.
- **Option 1 — Broad rollout:** Subsidise rapid deployment across eligible settings, including essential training and safety requirements.
- **Option 2 — Broad rollout with comprehensive workforce support:** Add protected learning time, staff consultation, role redesign and structured redeployment support.
- **Option 3 — Staged rollout with targeted support:** Begin with a limited cohort, independently assess outcomes and expand only when results justify further investment.
- Specify each option's beneficiaries, delivery mechanism, scale, timeframe and incremental public cost.
- Apply consistent criteria: efficiency, equity, feasibility and fiscal cost, with safety as a minimum requirement.
- Use an options-by-criteria table in the full draft, explaining trade-offs rather than assigning unsupported scores.
- Evaluate the costs of delaying useful adoption against the value of learning before wider investment.

## Recommended package and implementation — target 280 words

- Provisionally recommend option 3 with targeted workforce support.
- **Illustrative timeline, subject to feasibility:** Months 1–3 for procurement, baseline measurement and staff engagement; months 4–12 for implementation; months 13–15 for evaluation and an expansion decision.
- Propose DHSC sponsorship, participating trusts' operational delivery, staff and union involvement, and independent evaluation; verify institutional responsibilities.
- Set participant and site numbers after reviewing evidence and costs.
- Estimate costs in pounds sterling: licences, integration, training and staff cover, redeployment assistance, evaluation, administration and contingency.
- Show unit costs multiplied by programme scale; distinguish sourced prices from explicit planning assumptions.
- Measure net documentation time, cost per additional consultation, workload, overtime, retention and administrative staff outcomes.
- Check documentation quality and safety before any expansion.
- Use a comparison group or phased introduction where feasible; pre-specify expand, modify and stop criteria.
- Distinguish additional service capacity from cash savings; avoid double-counting benefits.

## Risks and mitigations — target 120 words

- Inaccurate documentation: clinician review, training and quality monitoring.
- Workforce displacement or work intensification: consultation, workload monitoring and redeployment support.
- Unequal organisational readiness: supported implementation and evaluation across different settings.
- Supplier dependence: assess interoperability and contractual exit provisions.
- Overstated benefits: independent evaluation, full cost accounting and sensitivity analysis.
- Slow adoption of useful technology: time-limited evaluation and explicit decision dates.
